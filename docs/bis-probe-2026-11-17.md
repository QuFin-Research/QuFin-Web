# BIS probe, 17 November 2026

What was read from the BIS Data Portal before eight new dataflows were wired in, and the choices
that followed. Every figure below was read live on 17 Nov 2026 from the SDMX API v2
(`https://stats.bis.org/api/v2`, keyless, `Accept: application/vnd.sdmx.data+json;version=1.0.0`),
about 60 requests in all, paced at least one second apart. Nothing was refused.

## How the data are fetched: API only, no bulk reader

The portal offers a zipped CSV per dataset at `data.bis.org/static/bulk/<flow>_csv_flat.zip`.
Sizes (compressed, read from the response headers):

| Flow | Bulk file | One keyed API request for the series QuFin keeps |
|---|---|---|
| `WS_TC` (total credit) | 1.8 MB | under 0.5 MB |
| `WS_CBTA` (central bank total assets) | 2.5 MB | under 0.5 MB |
| `WS_LONG_CPI` (consumer prices) | 0.9 MB | a few KB |
| `WS_NA_SEC_DSS` (debt securities) | 62.6 MB | under 1 MB |
| `WS_DEBT_SEC2_PUB` (international debt securities) | 147 MB | under 1 MB |

The existing connector already reads one measure per request with the economies joined by `+`, keeps
the raw response, and paces and stops on 403 or 429 (rule 28). A keyed request is small even for the
two large flows, so a bulk reader would add a second ingestion path (download, unzip, filter) to move
two hundred megabytes in order to keep a few dozen series. Not built. The one change to the
connector is the dataflow version: `WS_TC` is **2.0**, the others 1.0, so the version is now a
property of each measure.

### Choices inside each flow

- **Total credit.** Borrower `C` (all non-financial) is the private sector (`P`, already stored as
  the credit-to-GDP ratio from `WS_CREDIT_GAP`) plus the government, and `N` (non-financial
  corporations) is `P` minus households. Both are exactly derivable, so only households (`H`) and
  the government (`G`) are stored and the two derived ones are recorded in `REDUNDANT` (rule 16).
  Lenders all sectors, market value, ratio to GDP, adjusted for breaks.
- **Debt service ratios.** Private (`P`), households (`H`) and corporations (`N`) are separate
  indicators (a ratio of two sums is not the sum of the ratios), so all three are stored.
- **Global liquidity.** One series per economy: US-dollar credit (bank loans and debt securities)
  to non-bank borrowers located in it, in millions of dollars. The year-on-year version of the same
  series is derived, not stored. Regional and world aggregates have no country to attach to and are
  not stored.
- **Central bank total assets.** Quarterly, domestic currency, adjusted for breaks. The monthly
  series (48 economies) was loaded first and 33 of them were marked overdue by the release check:
  the BIS releases months only in quarterly batches (March, June, September, December), and the
  check expects a monthly series monthly. Widening the grace period is not allowed (rule 2), so the
  quarterly series, which has the BIS's own cadence and reaches 2026 Q2, replaced it; rule K
  (highest frequency) is not met for it. Israel, Indonesia and South Korea end a quarter earlier
  than the rest, and Kuwait has missing quarters: left out. It joins the existing `central_bank_balance_sheet` concept, so Fed
  total assets (FRED `WALCL`, weekly) and the BIS series of the other economies are one indicator
  (rule 19), compared as year-on-year change because the amounts are in different currencies.
- **International debt securities.** By issuer residence, all issuers, all currencies, amounts
  outstanding. Domestic debt securities (`WS_NA_SEC_DSS`) are a different, wider thing.
- **Debt securities.** Liabilities of general government, non-financial and financial corporations,
  all original maturities, nominal value, US dollars. Local-currency versions are derivable only
  with exchange rates and are not stored.
- **Commercial property.** The BIS holds 47 index series for 18 economies, with different property
  types, coverage and base years. One series per economy: whole country where it exists, the most
  general property type (all commercial, else offices and retail, offices, retail). Japan ends two
  quarters before the others, Greece is half-yearly, Poland and Portugal annual, Switzerland and the
  Czech Republic have only rented homes and land: none of these is stored.
- **Consumer prices.** The year-on-year rate, as the IMF series already stored are, so the concept
  has one shape. Only for economies no other source covers (the IMF came first, rule 16): of the 17
  without consumer prices the BIS has current series for New Zealand, Malta and Serbia (Kuwait's
  ends in June 2026 and was marked overdue, so it is out);
  its Chile and Colombia series were not taken because the IMF has both (loaded the same day, 442
  and 441 months); the United Arab Emirates ends in December 2025; Andorra, Cambodia, Cameroon,
  the Faroe Islands, the Isle of Man, Kosovo, Liechtenstein, Monaco, North Korea and Qatar are not
  in the BIS series.

### Left out of every new measure

- **The United States**, which FRED carries (old rule 17; the BIS US commercial property index was added on 19 Nov 2026 under rule P), and **Russia**, whose official data the BIS
  stopped receiving after 28 Feb 2022. The BIS has a US commercial property index (1990 to 2026 Q2);
  whether FRED has one could not be checked (the FRED key in this environment was rejected), so it
  stays out and is listed in the assumptions register.
- **Thailand, Peru, the Philippines and Morocco**, which are not in the taxonomy.
- **Economies with a gap or a stale series.** International debt securities for Oman, Congo, Kuwait,
  Kenya, Liechtenstein, Cambodia and Nigeria have missing quarters; Moldova's ends in 2002 and
  Iran's in 2009. Debt securities of non-financial corporations for Lithuania and Latvia have missing
  quarters. Kuwait's central bank total assets have missing quarters. A missing period is not
  filled (rule 3).
- **CPMI payments and FMIs** (eight annual tables, not a market indicator): not requested.

## Release dates

The BIS publishes its release calendar as a dataflow (`BIS_REL_CAL`, with dates into 2027), which
is how the lags below were read (period end to release date, across all listed dates):

| Series | Lag in days | Rule used in `bis_calendar.py` |
|---|---|---|
| Total credit, debt service, credit-to-GDP gaps | 156 to 171 | last weekday of the sixth month after the quarter (183 days), already used for credit |
| Debt securities | 156 to 171 | same |
| Global liquidity | 119 to 123 | same, later |
| Central bank total assets | 51 to 59, listed only for March, June, September and December | last weekday of the second month after the quarter (new `cbta_release`) |
| Consumer prices | 19 to 31 | last weekday of the second month after (new `cpi_release`), never early |
| Commercial property, international debt securities | no matching calendar entry | the quarterly rule, later than any observed lag |

A period QuFin first sees before its derived date is dated the day it is seen (rule 9).

## What was not verified

- **International debt securities units.** The flow publishes no unit attribute. Millions of US
  dollars follows the BIS's own description of the statistic; the US total (3,104,204 in 2026 Q2)
  is $3.1 trillion on that reading, which is the right size, but a changed unit would not be
  caught by the load.
- **Debt service ratio units.** No unit attribute either; percent of income is the BIS definition.
- **Whether FRED carries a US commercial property index** (see above).
- **Licence for these series.** Covered by the BIS terms already recorded (cite the BIS; no
  separate charge); the terms were read on 12 Nov 2026 and not re-read.
